AN ENTIRELY DIFFERENT KIND OF COMPUTER

Before circuits,
there were wheels.

The Engine separates what to do from the numbers it works on. That distinction is the key to understanding it.

  1. 01

    The cards give the instruction.

    Operation cards choose +, −, ×, or ÷. Variable cards select the columns that supply numbers and receive the answer.

  2. 02

    The store remembers.

    Vertical stacks of decimal wheels represent numbers. A value can be brought into the mill and retained for later use.

  3. 03

    The mill does the arithmetic.

    Two values enter. One operation is performed. The result returns to the store, ready for another card.

  4. 04

    A cycle makes the idea powerful.

    Cards can repeat a group of operations. In Note G, the same cycle combines successive Bernoulli numbers.

BUILT ON DOCUMENTS, GUIDED BY CURIOSITY

An honest reconstruction.

A.A.L.

This is a working logical reconstruction, informed by the 1843 account and Lovelace’s Notes. Babbage’s designs evolved, and a complete Analytical Engine was never built in his lifetime.

What follows the historical design?

The separate mill and store, four arithmetic operations, operation and variable cards, retained operands, and repeated card cycles. Note G follows the published table with documented corrections.

What is a modern interpretation?

The drawing, punched-hole patterns, animation, paired card display, pacing, and undo control are explanatory devices, not engineering plans. Exact fraction arithmetic is a teaching aid. Decimal mode uses 40 total digits with a chosen 20-place fractional scale and truncation after every operation. The scale is our convention; the 1843 account discusses 40 digits without fixing this display arrangement. Gear timing, carrying mechanisms, full card control, and physical tolerances are not simulated.

How is Ada’s table interpreted?

Note G’s reversed division at row 4 is corrected to (2n − 1)/(2n + 1). Row 24 explicitly negates the accumulated sum, following the governing equation. The program supplies earlier Bernoulli values and executes the repeated cycle. Ada’s B₁, B₃, B₅, B₇ correspond to modern B₂, B₄, B₆, B₈. This is not a verbatim punched-card transcript.
